Data Nerd Jobs: Market Analysis Dashboard

An interactive Power BI exploration of the global data job market, covering salaries, top skills, and geographic demand across roles.

Case Description

Aspiring data professionals need to understand the current market dynamics to optimize their study paths, target the right regions, and negotiate better salaries.

Based on Luke Barousse's comprehensive dataset, this project covers over 785,000 job postings from 172,000 companies across multiple countries, tracking real-world employer demands.

To uncover actionable career insights, the analysis was structured around 3 main objectives:

  • What are the most in-demand data roles and their geographic distribution?
  • What are the average salaries for different data professions?
  • Which technical skills are most critical for each specific job title?

Data Insights

1. Market Overview — Roles & Geography

The job market is heavily concentrated in core technical roles. Data Engineers and Data Analysts represent the vast majority of the postings, significantly outpacing generalized data science roles. Geographically, the United States dominates the volume of open positions, but there is a clear global footprint with strong activity in European and Asian tech hubs.

With a global average yearly salary sitting at $93.0K, the data indicates a highly lucrative field for those who can align their skills with employer expectations.

2. Salary Dynamics — Seniority & Titles

Not all data jobs are compensated equally. The analysis bucketed average salaries by job titles to identify where the highest financial returns are generated.

Senior Data Scientists and Data Scientists sit at the top of the compensation hierarchy, making them disproportionately profitable career endpoints. Data Engineers follow closely behind, while foundational roles like Data Analysts show a lower, but still highly competitive, baseline salary.

This hierarchy proves that moving from reporting (analysis) to architecture (engineering) or advanced predictive modeling (science) directly boosts earning potential.

3. The Skill Matrix — What Employers Want

Tool proficiency is the ultimate bottleneck. A matrix analysis of top skills mapped against job titles reveals exactly what foundational knowledge is required to enter the field.

SQL and Python are the undisputed kings, appearing as mandatory requirements across almost every single data role. For Data Analysts, Excel remains heavily utilized. Meanwhile, Data Engineers must pivot towards cloud infrastructure, with AWS appearing as a defining operational signal that separates them from analysts.

4. Dashboard Interactivity

The true power of this project lies in its dynamic filtering. By allowing users to slice the 785K records by Country, Job Title, and specific Skills, the dashboard transforms from a static report into a personalized career planning tool.

A user can instantly see how learning AWS impacts their salary potential in their specific home country, rounding out the picture of what separates generic advice from data-driven career strategy.
