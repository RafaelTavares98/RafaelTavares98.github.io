Chicago 311 Exploratory Analysis Case:

A Python-driven exploration of Chicago's 311 service requests, covering spatiotemporal patterns, geography, routing, and resolutions.

Case Description

The City of Chicago wants to understand the operational dynamics of its 311 system in order to improve response times, reduce misrouting, and optimize service efficiency.

The dataset covers millions of requests, tracking departments, and request types across city regions, spanning several years of city activity.

To uncover actionable insights, the analysis was structured around 3 main objectives:

  • What are the spatiotemporal patterns of the requests?
  • How are requests distributed across city geography?
  • What drives misrouting and how can it be resolved?

Data Insights

1. Spatiotemporal Patterns — Seasonality & Activity

The system's request volume shows clear annual seasonality, with most requests peaking during mid-year months and slowing down during winter. Daily activity is highly concentrated during weekdays and business hours, suggesting a predominantly routine-driven interaction with city services. The average response time varies by department, with the highest spikes recorded in specific categories, indicating some processing bottlenecks.

On the processing side, the average request is effectively tracked and categorized with a specific status, pointing to a moderately engaged and reasonably well-managed city service pool.

2. Geographic Distribution — Density & Clusters

Not all city areas generate equal volume. The analysis bucketed service requests by geographic coordinates, neighborhoods, and specific clusters to identify where real volume is generated.

High density clusters appear heavily in the northern half of the city and downtown, making them disproportionately active compared to the south. Similarly, specific clusters in the northwest relate to O'Hare Airport, while southwest clusters relate directly to Midway Airport.

The Inverse Pattern matters too: using "mean days open" shows the opposite—open-time spikes appear near the city limits rather than in high-volume central areas.

3. Data Relationships — Correct Routing & Misrouting

Routing is predominantly system-driven. A strong diagonal in the mapping of SR types to departments indicates correctly routed requests, followed closely by specific off-diagonal instances — a direct operational signal that misrouting and dispatch efficiency need improvement.

Citizens misclassify far less frequently, suggesting the core routing issue lies on the department overlap rather than user error. Clarifying categories through better form descriptions in high-volume requests could meaningfully cut misrouting rates.

4. Top Request Categories

The most frequent request types share a clear profile: they operate in high-density zones, affect public infrastructure directly, maintain a high daily volume rate, and tend to peak on specific seasons of the year. The analysis ranks all active categories by open-time duration — a metric that normalizes for request complexity and reflects true resolution efficiency.

Average closure time, department load, and geographic spread exposure round out the picture of what separates fast resolutions from the rest of the queue.
